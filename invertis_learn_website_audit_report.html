Solid Next.js foundation held back by empty resources and data drift

Live audit of invertislearn.vercel.app across 12 public routes on November 21, 2026. The portal delivers fast, login-free syllabus browsing, but advertised file counts, cross-page factual errors, and hidden search undermine student trust.

Bottom line: Keep the clean Next.js architecture, visual hierarchy, and frictionless student access. Fix three high-impact blockers before promoting the site wider: reconcile the 496+ Files claim with empty subject pages, correct factual errors across programme cards, and surface the 379-subject search tool plus Google indexing.

What Invertis Learn already gets right

Before fixing gaps, it is worth recognizing how much foundational engineering and interface work is already done well. The site behaves like a focused academic companion rather than a cluttered university portal.

Friction-free student access

Students can inspect full programme catalogs, semester breakdowns, credit structures, and subject pages without hitting a login wall. Client-side filters on /engineering and /search respond smoothly without full page reloads, and subject pages include quick anchor jumps for Syllabus PDF, PYQs, Notes, Books, and Video Lectures.

Cohesive design and stability

Across all 12 inspected routes, the Next.js app produced zero 404s, broken layouts, or runtime exceptions. The visual system pairs clean light surfaces with a clear slate-and-blue typographic hierarchy, monospace course codes, amber external ERP accents, and readable breadcrumbs.

Smart community upload workflow

On subject pages such as BAS101 Physics, students can contribute study notes via a well-structured upload modal (PDF up to 10 MB, 1–500 pages, optional credit attribution) without creating an account. Submissions route to a curator dashboard gated by a 32+ character key on /admin.

Engineering stability outpaces content completeness and data accuracy

Scoring six audit dimensions on a 10-point readiness rubric (derived directly from the 12-route inspection) shows a clear split: the codebase and visual system are near production quality, while data hygiene, populated study materials, and search discoverability lag far behind.

Content gaps and cross-page contradictions undermine student trust

For an academic portal, accuracy matters more than visual polish. Several high-visibility numbers and programme facts currently contradict one another across pages, which can make students assume the site is unfinished or unreliable.

UI bugs, hidden search, and legal UX friction slow down everyday use

None of these defects break the application, but together they make navigation harder than it needs to be—especially for first-time students looking for a specific subject code before an exam.

  • Promote the hidden 379-subject search bar: The global search page (/search) is one of the portal's most useful features, yet it is only linked in the footer as Find subjects. Adding a search icon or Find Subjects link directly in the top navigation bar—and a quick-search input on the homepage hero—will cut clicks dramatically.
  • Remove /admin from the public footer and clean up dead labels: Exposing Curator review (/admin) in the global student footer adds clutter and invites curiosity clicks. Similarly, All University Departments (Coming Soon) sits in the navigation column as plain non-clickable text and has low contrast against the dark #0F172A footer.
  • Fix horizontal tab clipping on /phd: On /phd, the tab bar clips the final tab (Thesis & Viv...) even on a standard desktop viewport with no scroll indicator or wrapping. Allow tabs to wrap cleanly or add horizontal scroll controls with visible fade cues for tablet and mobile screens.
  • Tighten card badges, placeholders, and click targets: Shorten the truncated search placeholder on /engineering (currently cut off at "Search CSE program, code, or specializ"), remove the duplicate BTECH-019 badge on BTECH-CSE-CC, prevent two-line wrapping on Resources Live and Course Breakdown labels, and make entire subject cards on semester grids clickable rather than only the Explore -> link.
  • Uncheck the upload copyright confirmation by default: Inside the community upload modal on BAS101, the legal confirmation checkbox ("I confirm that I have the right to share this educational material...") is pre-ticked. Pre-checked consent boxes weaken copyright compliance; leave it unchecked and require an explicit click before enabling submission.

Zero Google indexing leaves subject pages invisible to organic search

A direct search for site:invertislearn.vercel.app returned zero indexed pages on Google as of November 21, 2026. Students searching Google for "Invertis University BAS101 syllabus" or "Invertis B.Tech CSE PYQ" cannot currently discover the portal at all.

Technical SEO essentials to add next

Publish a dynamic sitemap.xml covering all 379 subject routes and programme pages, verify robots.txt allows crawling (while blocking /admin), and generate unique <title> and meta descriptions per subject code (e.g., "BAS101 Physics Syllabus, PYQs & Notes — Invertis University B.Tech CSE"). Register the site in Google Search Console and consider a memorable custom domain so students trust it as an official or established campus resource.

High-leverage features students will actually use

Once core data is clean, a few lightweight additions can turn Invertis Learn from a static directory into a daily study habit: localStorage-based "My Semester" pinning (no login needed), inline PDF preview before downloading, a "Request PYQ / Notes" counter per subject so curators know which exams to prioritize, and a clear "Last Updated" timestamp on every syllabus and resource section.

The fastest way to level up Invertis Learn isn't rebuilding the UI—it's making every badge, file count, and semester label tell the exact truth.
